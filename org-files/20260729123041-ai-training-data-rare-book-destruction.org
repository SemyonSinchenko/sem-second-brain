#+title: AI training data: rare book destruction
#+ROAM_REFS: https://gardinerbryant.com/ai-companies-are-now-destroying-warehouses-full-of-rare-books/
#+filetags: :article:

* Summary
Source: [[https://gardinerbryant.com/ai-companies-are-now-destroying-warehouses-full-of-rare-books/][https://gardinerbryant.com/ai-companies-are-now-destroying-warehouses-full-of-rare-books/]]
Investigates allegations that AI companies are purchasing rare books, scanning them for [[id:96a58b04-1f58-47c9-993f-551994939252][LLM]] training datasets, and subsequently destroying the physical copies to avoid resale or detection.

* Key Takeaways
- Physical books are treated as disposable data containers rather than cultural artifacts.
- Destruction prevents secondary market sales which could reveal the scale of data acquisition.
- Raises significant ethical questions regarding copyright and cultural preservation.
- The practice suggests a high demand for high-quality, non-digital text data for model training.

* Notes
This practice highlights the extreme measures taken to secure high-quality text data for model training. The destruction of source material poses a risk to historical preservation. The workflow typically involves:
1. Bulk purchasing of rare or out-of-print books.
2. High-speed scanning for digitization.
3. Pulping or shredding the physical copies.

This connects to broader discussions on data provenance and the environmental/ethical cost of AI development.
